How AI Is Changing the Shopping Journey: From ChatGPT Research to Google Validation

What the widely shared 99 percent statistic really says, what independent behavioral data shows and how brands can win across AI discovery, search validation and website conversion.

The shopping journey is not moving from Google to ChatGPT in one clean switch. It is becoming a connected sequence. A shopper may ask an AI assistant to explain the category, use Google to test the shortlist, visit retailer and brand pages to verify the details, and then buy through whichever channel feels most trustworthy and convenient.

The Short Answer

AI is becoming a powerful discovery and research layer, but it has not removed search or the website from the decision. The winning strategy is not SEO or GEO. Brands need accurate information, credible third-party signals and a persuasive website that works consistently across AI answers, Google results and product pages.

What the Latest Shopping Data Actually Shows

A conference slide shared from Google's Rethink Retail event triggered the discussion. It stated that 99 percent of shoppers who research with ChatGPT consult Google for final decisions. The small print matters. The figure came from a Google-commissioned Ipsos Consumer Continuous online survey conducted in April 2026 among 12,038 ChatGPT commercial users aged 16 to 74. It represented a global average across selected countries and was not weighted to reflect population size.

The same event presented three related findings: 81 percent of people validate discoveries from social platforms on Google Search, AI users encounter 2.8 times more touchpoints than non-AI users, and two out of three people arriving at Google from social channels eventually buy a different brand. These figures point to a fragmented journey where discovery does not equal selection.

Adobe's behavioral data helps test the direction of the survey claims. Its March 2025 analysis covered more than one trillion visits to US retail sites and a companion survey of more than 5,000 consumers. AI referral traffic had risen 1,200 percent between July 2024 and February 2025. These visitors viewed 12 percent more pages and bounced 23 percent less than visitors from other sources, but converted 9 percent less often. That conversion gap had narrowed from 43 percent in July 2024.

By the 2025 holiday season, Adobe reported a different picture. AI referral traffic was up 693.4 percent year over year, AI visitors converted 31 percent better than other traffic, spent 45 percent longer on retail sites, viewed 13 percent more pages and were 33 percent less likely to leave immediately. This progression supports a reasonable interpretation: AI started as a research tool, then became a source of increasingly purchase-ready traffic.

Can We Trust the 99 Percent Claim?

The figure is useful, but it needs disciplined wording. It does not mean 99 percent of all shoppers use ChatGPT, nor does it show that Google caused the final purchase. It means that within a defined group of surveyed ChatGPT commercial users, almost everyone said they consulted Google at the final-decision stage.

A trustworthy way to cite the number: A Google-commissioned Ipsos online survey of 12,038 ChatGPT commercial users across selected global markets reported that 99 percent consulted Google for final purchase decisions. The result is self-reported and should be read as evidence of cross-channel behavior, not a universal observed conversion rate.

Three factors deserve attention. First, Google commissioned the study and presented the result at its own retail event. Second, online surveys measure what participants remember and report, which may differ from clickstream or transaction data. Third, the sample included commercial users of ChatGPT, not every internet user. The large sample improves precision within the surveyed population, but it does not remove those design limitations.

The best conclusion comes from triangulation. Google and Ipsos show stated cross-channel behavior. Adobe shows rapid growth and improving conversion among measurable AI referrals. A 2026 study using activity from 31 million users of Ctrip found that shopping chat and conventional search commonly appeared in the same journey and were often interleaved. Together, the evidence supports complementarity more strongly than replacement.

The New AI Shopping Journey

Traditional funnels imply a tidy sequence from awareness to consideration to purchase. Real digital journeys already looped between ads, social media, search, reviews, marketplaces and websites. Generative AI adds another interface that can compress research without eliminating the need to verify current commercial facts.

Stage 1NeedThe buyer describes a problem, context or goal.
Stage 2AI DiscoveryAn assistant explains options and creates a shortlist.
Stage 3Search ValidationThe buyer checks alternatives, reputation and recent evidence.
Stage 4Website ConfirmationCurrent product, policy and business details reduce risk.
Stage 5PurchaseThe buyer chooses the most credible and convenient channel.

AI Is Strongest at Exploratory Questions

A person can ask, “Which type of laptop suits a travelling designer who edits video twice a week?” That is harder to express as a short keyword. AI can ask follow-up questions, explain tradeoffs and turn an uncertain need into evaluation criteria. Adobe's consumer survey found that among people using generative AI for shopping, 55 percent used it for research, 47 percent for recommendations and 43 percent for finding deals.

Search Is Strongest at Open-Ended Verification

Once a shopper has a shortlist, the queries become more specific: brand reviews, product name problems, price comparisons, alternatives, returns, warranty, delivery location and seller reputation. Search lets the person choose the source, compare recent pages and inspect multiple perspectives. Google's own AI results may participate, but the user is still validating through a wider information environment.

The Website Resolves Commercial Uncertainty

The brand or retailer website should provide the current truth about the offer. That includes the exact model, variant, stock, price, tax, shipping promise, return conditions, warranty and support route. It must also explain why this product and this seller are the right choice. A website is therefore both a confirmation step and a persuasion step.

Why Shoppers Verify AI Recommendations

Information Can Be Stale

Price, inventory, delivery dates and product versions change faster than many summaries. Buyers need a current commercial source.

Recommendations Can Conflict

Different assistants may prioritize different sources, criteria or products. A second channel helps buyers understand the disagreement.

High-Risk Purchases Need Proof

Costly, technical or personal purchases create more questions about compatibility, warranty, safety and long-term value.

The Seller Still Owns the Promise

An AI assistant does not normally deliver the parcel, process the return or provide the warranty. The buyer needs confidence in the merchant.

Verification is not evidence that AI failed. It can be the natural next action after AI succeeds in narrowing the field. A helpful answer reduces hundreds of possibilities to three. Search and the website then help the shopper choose between those three using current facts and personally trusted evidence.

The Website Must Confirm and Persuade

The statement that a website is “no longer the persuasion step” is memorable, but too absolute. Confirmation can itself be persuasive. Clear delivery dates reduce uncertainty. A candid limitation builds credibility. A strong comparison helps the visitor justify a choice. A useful calculator makes value concrete. These elements confirm facts while changing the buyer's confidence.

The site also controls details that an AI summary may omit: which customer should not buy the product, what is included, what happens after purchase, how support works and why the company can be trusted. Brands that treat product pages as thin checkout screens waste the intent created by both AI and search.

Fourteen Website Signals That Help Confirm a Purchase

  1. Accurate price: show the current price, currency, tax treatment and any genuine conditions.
  2. Live availability: distinguish in stock, back order, preorder and unavailable states.
  3. Specific delivery information: provide realistic destinations, costs and time ranges before checkout.
  4. Clear product identity: state model, variant, size, color, version and package contents without ambiguity.
  5. Complete specifications: publish measurable details that support side-by-side evaluation.
  6. Original images and video: show the real product, scale, use and important details rather than relying only on supplier media.
  7. Evidence-led benefits: connect each major claim to a feature, test, certification or demonstrated use.
  8. Honest limitations: explain who the product does not suit and which tradeoffs the buyer accepts.
  9. Useful reviews: make recent, specific and verifiable customer feedback easy to inspect.
  10. Returns information: state the period, condition, cost, process and exclusions in plain language.
  11. Warranty details: identify the responsible party, coverage, duration and claim route.
  12. Business identity: make the company, location, contact information and support channels visible.
  13. Secure, usable checkout: remove surprise fees, broken fields and unnecessary account friction.
  14. Accessible support: offer a realistic path for pre-purchase questions and post-purchase problems.

These are not cosmetic additions. They answer the questions a shopper is likely to carry from AI and Google into the page. They also create structured, quotable and consistent information that search systems and AI assistants can understand.

Build Visibility Across SEO and AI Search

A brand cannot control every AI recommendation, but it can improve the information environment that models and shoppers encounter. Begin with crawlable pages, consistent entities, accurate product feeds, useful structured data and original content. Then strengthen third-party corroboration through expert coverage, genuine customer discussion, trusted directories, partnerships and editorial references.

Technical Foundations for Product Discovery

  • Allow appropriate search and AI crawlers to access valuable public content.
  • Use Product, Offer, Review and Organization structured data only when it matches visible information.
  • Keep canonical URLs, variants, pagination and faceted navigation under control.
  • Maintain accurate product feeds and synchronize price and stock.
  • Give important product pages stable internal links from categories, guides and comparisons.
  • Use descriptive text around images and provide relevant alternative text.
  • Keep policy, contact and business information easy to find.

Search infrastructure is also changing. Google's use of redirect URLs and its partner-only search services show how access, measurement and result delivery can evolve. Our research on Google goto URLs and the Google Web Search Service API provides additional context for technical teams.

The strongest content does not repeat a manufacturer description in longer form. It contributes something observable: original measurements, images, expert judgment, compatibility data, real examples, a transparent methodology or a tool that helps the reader decide. This improves usefulness for people and gives other publishers a reason to reference the page.

How to Measure a Cross-Channel Shopping Journey

Last-click reporting will miss much of this behavior. A person may research on a personal ChatGPT account, use Google on a phone, return directly on a laptop and buy later. Some AI answers generate no click at all. The goal is not perfect attribution. It is enough evidence to make better decisions.

  1. Separate known AI referrals: create a channel group for traffic from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot, Gemini and other identifiable assistants.
  2. Track landing-page quality: compare engagement, product progression, conversion and revenue rather than visit volume alone.
  3. Monitor branded search: increases in brand plus product queries can reflect discovery that occurred elsewhere.
  4. Measure assisted journeys: use analytics path reports and reasonable lookback windows to identify repeat visits.
  5. Add post-purchase questions: ask where the customer first discovered the product and which sources helped the decision.
  6. Record AI visibility: test a stable set of commercial prompts and note mentions, citations, sentiment and accuracy.
  7. Audit information consistency: compare what AI says with the product page, feed, policies and third-party listings.
  8. Watch business outcomes: conversion, contribution, returns, support demand and repeat purchase matter more than citation count alone.
Measurement warning: AI referral growth can look dramatic because the starting volume is small. Always show both the percentage change and the absolute visits, conversions and revenue.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do 99 percent of ChatGPT shoppers use Google before buying?

A Google-commissioned Ipsos online survey reported that 99 percent of 12,038 ChatGPT commercial users across selected markets consulted Google for final decisions. It is a large self-reported survey, but it should not be interpreted as observed behavior for every shopper.

Is ChatGPT replacing Google for product research?

Current evidence suggests the channels are complementary. AI helps people explore and narrow choices, while Google and retailer websites help verify current price, stock, reviews, policies and business legitimacy.

What is an AI shopping journey?

It is a customer path where an AI assistant supports discovery, research, comparison or recommendation before the shopper verifies details and completes a purchase through search, a marketplace, a retailer or a brand website.

Why do shoppers verify AI recommendations?

Commercial facts change and AI answers can be incomplete. Buyers often verify specifications, price, stock, delivery, reviews, returns, warranty and seller legitimacy, especially for expensive or complex products.

Does a website only confirm an AI recommendation?

No. It confirms important facts and persuades through positioning, evidence, presentation, service quality and reduced risk. Confirmation and persuasion usually work together.

How should ecommerce brands optimize for AI search?

Publish accurate product data, original evidence, clear comparisons, concise answers, expert guidance and transparent policies. Keep the site crawlable, use appropriate structured data and maintain consistent information across trusted sources.

Which trust signals matter most after AI discovery?

Important signals include accurate price and stock, clear specifications, delivery estimates, returns and warranty terms, useful reviews, original media, business identity, secure checkout and accessible support.

How can marketers measure the AI shopping journey?

Track identifiable AI referrals, assisted conversions, branded search, landing-page engagement, return visits and conversion by source. Add customer surveys because cross-device and zero-click journeys often cannot be reconstructed from analytics alone.

The Website Is the Evidence Layer and the Experience Layer

AI can shorten discovery, but it does not remove uncertainty. Search, reviews, marketplaces and brand websites help people test recommendations against recent evidence and personal risk. As assistants gain checkout capabilities, some steps may compress further, but accurate product data, credible proof and reliable fulfilment will still determine whether a recommendation produces a satisfied customer.

The strategic response is simple but demanding. Be discoverable where questions begin, be verifiable where shoppers investigate and be convincing where the transaction happens. Brands that coordinate those three jobs will be better prepared than brands chasing AI mentions without improving the underlying customer experience.
