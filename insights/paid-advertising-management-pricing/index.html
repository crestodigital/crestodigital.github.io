Paid Advertising Management Pricing: Agency Fees and Ad Costs

A clear 2026 guide to what businesses pay for Google, Meta, Microsoft, LinkedIn, TikTok and Amazon advertising management.

By Cresto Digital25 August 202619-minute readPricing checked August 2026

The price of paid advertising is easy to misunderstand because a proposal can contain several different numbers. The media budget buys attention from an advertising platform. The management fee pays the people responsible for turning that budget into qualified traffic, leads or revenue.

Short answer

Paid advertising management commonly costs from $500 to $5,000 or more per month for small and mid-sized accounts. Another common model charges roughly 10 to 20 percent of monthly ad spend, usually with a minimum fee. Setup, tracking, creative production, landing pages and software may be quoted separately. These are indicative market ranges in US dollars, not official fees set by advertising platforms.

Pricing varies widely by market, agency position, platform mix and scope. Published market surveys show broad ranges rather than a universal rate. For example, a Credo PPC pricing survey found that many providers charged $50 to $200 per hour and that retainer minimums frequently sat between $1,000 and $3,000.

Advertising spend

This is the money used to buy media from Google, Meta, Microsoft, LinkedIn, TikTok, Amazon or another network. It determines how many auctions the business can enter, but it does not guarantee a fixed number of clicks or sales. Results still depend on the auction, targeting, creative, offer, website and competition.

Platforms control spend differently. Google Ads uses an average daily budget and may spend more on high-opportunity days while staying within its monthly charging rules. Google explains that most campaigns can spend up to twice the average daily budget on a particular day, while the monthly charging limit is generally 30.4 times that daily budget. See Google’s official guide to managing Google Ads spend.

Management fee

The management fee pays for judgement and ongoing work. That may include market research, campaign architecture, keyword and audience development, conversion tracking, budget pacing, search-term reviews, bid decisions, creative testing, reporting and recommendations. A fee should reflect the actual operating scope, not simply the number of ads created on day one.

Common paid advertising pricing models

No single model is best for every account. The fair model is the one that reflects workload, risk and expected service while keeping incentives understandable.

Predictable

Fixed monthly retainer

The business pays an agreed amount for a defined monthly scope.

  • Good for stable budgets and services
  • Easy to forecast
  • Needs clear limits for campaigns, platforms and meetings
Scalable

Percentage of ad spend

The fee rises and falls with the media budget. A common market range is about 10 to 20 percent.

  • Can reflect added responsibility as spend grows
  • Often includes a minimum monthly fee
  • Needs safeguards so higher spend is not the only incentive
Launch

Setup fee plus monthly management

A one-time fee covers foundational work before recurring optimisation begins.

  • Useful for new or poorly configured accounts
  • Can cover tracking, research and campaign construction
  • The proposal should state what becomes the client’s property
Flexible

Hourly consulting

An expert charges for audits, troubleshooting, training or limited support.

  • Useful for in-house teams
  • Good for short, defined projects
  • Less predictable for continuous management
Outcome-linked

Performance-based fee

Part or all of the fee is tied to leads, sales, revenue or another agreed outcome.

  • Can align commercial incentives
  • Requires trusted attribution and lead-quality rules
  • Risky when the agency does not control the offer or sales process
Balanced

Hybrid model

A base retainer is combined with a smaller spend percentage or performance incentive.

  • Provides a minimum service level
  • Can adapt as the account scales
  • Must remain simple enough to audit

Paid advertising management costs by platform

Platform choice changes the work behind the fee. Search campaigns require query and keyword control. Social campaigns depend more heavily on creative volume and audience testing. Marketplace advertising adds product feeds, catalogue economics and retail operations.

Intent-led

Google Ads

Typical management: $500 to $5,000+ monthly or about 10 to 20 percent of spend.

  • Search, Shopping, Display, Performance Max and YouTube
  • Keyword strategy, search terms, feeds, bidding and conversion tracking
  • Complexity rises with locations, products and campaign types
Creative-led

Meta Ads

Typical management: $500 to $3,500+ monthly or about 10 to 20 percent of spend.

  • Facebook and Instagram placements
  • Audience, catalogue, creative and landing page testing
  • Production volume can cost more than media management
Search

Microsoft Advertising

Typical management: $300 to $2,000+ monthly, often bundled with Google Ads.

  • Search, shopping and audience campaigns
  • Useful as an incremental search channel
  • Importing campaigns still requires tracking and query review
B2B

LinkedIn Ads

Typical management: $1,000 to $5,000+ monthly.

  • Professional audience and account targeting
  • Lead forms, thought leader ads and website conversion campaigns
  • Higher media costs make offer and lead quality critical
Video-first

TikTok Ads

Typical management: $750 to $4,000+ monthly, excluding production.

  • Rapid creative testing and frequent refreshes
  • Creator sourcing and editing may be separate
  • Success depends on platform-native video rather than simple media buying
Retail media

Amazon Ads

Typical management: $750 to $5,000+ monthly or about 10 to 20 percent of spend.

  • Sponsored Products, Brands and Display
  • Search terms, bids, product detail pages and retail readiness
  • Fees may include catalogue or marketplace operations
Important: platform management ranges overlap because scope matters more than the logo on the dashboard. Managing one mature campaign is not equivalent to launching multiple countries, catalogues, funnels and creative systems.

How much ad budget does a small business need?

Start from the economics of the sale, not from a generic budget table. Estimate the target number of new customers, acceptable acquisition cost, lead-to-sale rate and landing page conversion rate. Then calculate the clicks and spend required to test those assumptions.

For example, if a business wants 10 customers, closes 20 percent of qualified leads and expects the landing page to convert 5 percent of clicks, it needs about 50 leads and roughly 1,000 clicks. If an estimated click costs $4, the test budget is around $4,000 before management. Real results will differ, but the calculation exposes whether the plan is commercially plausible.

What a paid ads management fee should include

Strategy and research

  • Commercial goals and KPI definition
  • Market and competitor review
  • Keyword, audience and funnel research
  • Channel and budget recommendation

Campaign foundations

  • Account and campaign structure
  • Conversion tracking validation
  • Audience and exclusion setup
  • Feed or catalogue configuration where needed

Creative and landing input

  • Ad copy and message testing
  • Creative briefs and variation planning
  • Landing page recommendations
  • Offer and conversion-path review

Ongoing optimisation

  • Search-term and placement reviews
  • Bid and budget adjustments
  • Negative keywords and exclusions
  • Creative, audience and campaign testing

Measurement

  • Lead, sale and revenue reporting
  • Cost per acquisition and return analysis
  • Data-quality checks
  • Clear commentary and next actions

Communication

  • Agreed reporting cadence
  • Named account owner
  • Change history and test record
  • Escalation path for urgent issues

Do not assume every item is included. Custom landing pages, video production, photography, feed tools, call tracking, analytics engineering and customer relationship management integrations are commonly priced separately.

Should the client pay the platforms directly?

In most relationships, yes. The business should own its advertising account, billing profile, pixels, conversion data, product catalogues and related business assets. The agency should work through the platform’s manager, partner or role-based access system.

A clean account governance model

The client owns
  • Advertising accounts and historical data
  • Billing methods and platform payments
  • Pixels, tags, audiences and catalogues
  • Primary administrative control
The agency receives
  • Manager or partner access
  • The minimum permissions required
  • Access to approved analytics and business assets
  • Removal when the engagement ends

This structure protects continuity. Google explains that linked manager access does not remove the client account’s data rights, and Microsoft explicitly recommends that clients create their own accounts before linking an agency. LinkedIn also provides distinct account roles for campaign, account and billing responsibilities.

What changes an agency management fee?

  1. Number of platforms: each platform adds setup, optimisation, policy and reporting work.
  2. Campaign complexity: multiple markets, products, languages, locations and funnels need more control.
  3. Ad spend: larger budgets increase pacing, testing and commercial responsibility, even when the percentage rate falls.
  4. Creative demand: social and video campaigns may need a continuous production pipeline.
  5. Tracking maturity: server-side tracking, offline conversion imports and CRM integration add technical scope.
  6. Landing page work: design, copy, development and experimentation may be separate services.
  7. Meeting and reporting cadence: weekly executive reporting takes more time than a standard monthly review.
  8. Industry risk: regulated sectors, sensitive categories and complex approval processes need additional care.

Costs that may sit outside the quoted management fee

  • Initial account audit and campaign rebuild
  • New landing page design and development
  • Photography, design and video production
  • Feed management and marketplace operations
  • Call tracking and paid reporting software
  • CRM, analytics and offline conversion integration
  • Taxes and platform billing charges
  • Rush work or additional strategy meetings

How to choose a paid advertising agency

Price matters, but the cheapest management fee can become expensive if tracking is unreliable, spend is poorly controlled or the team reports clicks without connecting them to qualified leads and revenue.

Questions to ask before signing

  • Will our business own every advertising account?
  • Is media spend paid directly to the platform?
  • What is included in setup and monthly management?
  • Which services are billed separately?
  • Who will work on the account after the sales process?
  • How often will campaigns be reviewed?
  • How will conversion tracking be tested?
  • Which commercial KPIs will guide decisions?
  • How are creative tests planned and recorded?
  • What happens when a campaign underperforms?
  • Can we see a clear change history?
  • What is the notice period and handover process?

Warning signs in a proposal

  • Ad spend and management fees are blended into one unexplained number
  • The agency insists on owning the client’s advertising accounts
  • Guaranteed revenue is promised without validated funnel data
  • Reports focus on impressions and clicks but ignore qualified outcomes
  • No one is responsible for conversion tracking quality
  • Creative, landing page and software costs appear only after signing
  • The scope does not define platforms, campaigns or meeting cadence
  • There is no handover process for data, assets and access

Frequently asked questions

How much does paid advertising management cost?

Many small and mid-sized accounts encounter fees from $500 to $5,000 or more per month. Agencies may also charge around 10 to 20 percent of ad spend, often with a minimum monthly fee. The actual price depends on platforms, campaigns, markets, creative and tracking scope.

Do agency fees include ad spend?

Usually no. The client pays the advertising platform for media, while the agency charges separately for management. Ask for both amounts to appear as separate lines.

What percentage do PPC agencies charge?

A common published market range is about 10 to 20 percent of monthly ad spend. Lower-spend accounts may face a minimum fee or a higher percentage. Large accounts may negotiate lower tiered percentages.

Do agencies charge a setup fee?

Many do. Setup can include an audit, tracking configuration, research, campaign structure, audiences, feeds and initial creative planning. The proposal should define the deliverables and ownership.

Should the client own the ad account?

Yes, in most cases. The client should keep ownership of accounts, billing and data. The agency should receive manager or partner access appropriate to the work.

Is a freelancer cheaper than an agency?

A freelancer may charge less because overhead is lower. An agency may provide broader expertise across media buying, creative, analytics and landing pages. Compare the people, scope, response time and accountability, not only the headline price.

Is performance-based pricing better?

It can work when attribution, lead quality and sales responsibility are clearly defined. It becomes difficult when the agency cannot control the website, offer, stock, pricing or sales follow-up.

How much should a small business spend on ads?

Work backwards from customer value, target acquisition cost, sales close rate and website conversion rate. A budget should be large enough to test a meaningful number of clicks or prospects without placing the business under financial pressure.

Is paid advertising cheaper than SEO?

They buy different things. Paid media can produce immediate visibility while the campaign is funded. SEO builds organic visibility over time. Compare the cost and return of each channel against the same commercial outcome.

Judge the fee by the system behind it

A management fee is not valuable because an agency changes bids or publishes reports. It is valuable when the team connects media, creative, measurement and landing page decisions to a commercial goal.

Ask for a proposal that separates ad spend, management, setup, creative and technical work. Keep ownership of the accounts and data. Agree on the KPIs, reporting rhythm and handover process before campaigns begin. That makes a low, medium or premium fee much easier to judge.
