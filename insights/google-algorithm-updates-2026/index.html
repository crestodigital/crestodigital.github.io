Google Algorithm Updates 2026: Complete Timeline, August Spam Update & What Changed for SEO

Google has rolled out multiple core and spam updates in 2026 while also changing Discover, AI Search guidance, spam policies and structured data features. This guide separates confirmed changes from speculation.

By Cresto Digital SEO TeamPublished: August 21, 2026Updated: August 21, 2026
Quick answer: 2026 has not been one single Google update. The major confirmed ranking rollouts include a February Discover core update, March spam and core updates, a May core update, a June spam update and the August 2026 spam update. Google has also published major AI Search guidance and made important spam-policy and structured-data changes.
August 2026 spam update status: Google began the rollout on August 18, 2026 at about 9:27 a.m. Pacific Time. It applies globally and to all languages. As of August 21, current official and reliable industry reporting still describes the rollout as ongoing, with Google saying it may take a few days to complete.

February 2026 Discover Core Update

Google released a dedicated Discover core update on February 5. Google said it was designed to show more locally relevant content from websites based in the user's country, reduce sensational content and clickbait, and surface more original, timely and in-depth content from sites with expertise in a topic.

A particularly useful clarification was that Google can understand expertise on a topic-by-topic basis. A site does not need to publish about only one subject to demonstrate expertise.

Read Google's Discover update announcement.

March 2026 Spam Update

The March spam update started on March 24 and finished in about 19.5 hours. It applied globally and to all languages. Google did not announce a new spam category alongside the rollout, so it is safer to treat it as an improvement to existing automated spam-detection systems rather than inventing a new March-specific target.

March 2026 Core Update

The March core update began on March 27 and completed on April 8. A core update is not the same thing as a spam update.

Core update

Broad changes to how Google evaluates usefulness, relevance and quality. A legitimate site can lose rankings without violating a spam policy.

Spam update

Improvements to systems that identify behavior violating Google's spam policies. A sustained loss calls for a policy-compliance review.

Google's core update guidance.

April 2026: Back Button Hijacking Became an Explicit Spam Violation

On April 13, Google announced that back button hijacking would become an explicit violation under its malicious-practices spam policy. The technique interferes with browser history so users cannot simply return to the page they came from. Google said affected pages may face manual actions or automated demotions, with enforcement beginning June 15.

Read Google's policy announcement.

May 2026 Core Update

The second broad core update of the year began on May 21 and completed on June 2. Google did not publish a list of individual ranking factors that changed. Claims such as “Google reduced backlinks by X%” or “content length became more important” should therefore be treated cautiously unless supported by reliable evidence.

May 2026: Major Google Guidance for AI Overviews and AI Mode

On May 15, Google published dedicated guidance for generative AI features such as AI Overviews and AI Mode. This was one of the most important SEO developments of 2026.

Traditional SEO still matters

Google says foundational SEO best practices remain relevant to generative AI Search. Pages still need to be crawlable, indexable, understandable and relevant.

Google confirmed query fan-out

Google's AI Search documentation describes query fan-out, where the model can generate several related searches to collect the information needed for a broader answer. This increases the importance of complete semantic coverage, but Google warns against creating separate pages for every possible query variation mainly to manipulate rankings or generative responses.

Non-commodity content is becoming more important

Google explicitly encourages unique, valuable and non-commodity content. The practical advantage comes from adding first-hand experience, original data, testing, expert interpretation, useful images, videos and a distinctive point of view rather than simply rewriting information already available across the web.

Weak scaled-AI workflow

  • Hundreds of keyword variations
  • Near-identical templates
  • Generic summaries of existing results
  • No original evidence or experience
  • Published mainly to capture search traffic

Stronger AI-assisted workflow

  • Research and expert input first
  • Original examples, screenshots or data
  • Clear user benefit
  • Human fact-checking and editing
  • AI used as an assistant, not the source of all value

Google's generative AI Search optimization guide.

Spam Policies Now Apply to Generative AI Responses in Search

Google also clarified in May that its spam policies apply to attempts to manipulate generative AI responses in Search. That means scaled content abuse is relevant not only to traditional blue links, but also to AI Overviews and AI Mode.

Important: Google does not say AI-generated content is automatically spam. The problem is scaled, unoriginal or manipulative content created primarily to influence Search, regardless of whether it was made with AI, scraping, templates or manual processes.

May–June 2026: FAQ Rich Results Were Retired

Google deprecated the FAQ rich-result feature in May and removed the related documentation in June. FAQ sections can still be useful for users and topical coverage, but site owners should no longer expect FAQPage markup to produce the old expandable FAQ result.

Preferred Sources Expanded to AI Overviews and AI Mode

Google updated Preferred Sources documentation in May to include AI Overviews and AI Mode. This reinforces the value of brand recognition and becoming a source users actively prefer, not just winning isolated keyword rankings.

June 2026 Spam Update

The June spam update started on June 24 and completed on June 26. It applied worldwide and across all languages. As with March, Google did not attach a new spam-policy category to the rollout.

June 2026: Google Clarified llms.txt

On June 15, Google clarified that llms.txt is not required for Google Search and does not positively or negatively affect Google Search visibility or rankings. You can maintain the file for other systems, but it should not be presented as a Google ranking shortcut or a requirement for AI Overviews.

July 2026: Review Snippet Guidance Tightened

On July 24, Google added guidance covering fake and undisclosed incentivized reviews. Reviews should be based on genuine experience, and incentives such as money, discounts, vouchers or free products should be clearly disclosed.

August 2026 Spam Update: What We Know

Google began rolling out the August 2026 spam update on August 18 at roughly 9:27 a.m. Pacific Time. It applies globally and to all languages and is the third spam update of 2026.

Google did not announce a new spam policy

There was no new August-specific spam category. That means claims that the update is specifically a “link spam update,” an “AI content update,” a “site reputation update,” or a “Claude watermark update” should not be presented as confirmed facts.

Confirmed: global rollout, all languages, standard spam update, third spam update of 2026.

Not confirmed: a specific focus on AI-written articles, ChatGPT, Claude, AI watermarks, link spam or any one spam tactic.

Does the August update target AI content?

Google has not said AI-generated content is the specific target. However, Google's existing scaled content abuse policy clearly covers mass-produced low-value content created primarily to manipulate Search. A site publishing hundreds of near-identical AI pages can therefore be vulnerable without Google needing to identify which AI model wrote them.

Does Google detect Claude or ChatGPT watermarks?

There is no reliable public evidence showing that Google uses a Claude- or ChatGPT-specific text watermark as a direct indexing or ranking signal. Google may use undisclosed classifiers and automated quality signals, but that is different from proving a specific watermark-based indexing mechanism.

The safer SEO assumption is: act as if Google can recognize highly automated, repetitive and generic content very well, then make the page valuable even if Google knows AI assisted with it.

What SEOs Should Check After the 2026 Updates

  1. Annotate confirmed rollout dates. Compare Search Console data before, during and after each window.
  2. Segment the loss. Review pages, queries, directories, countries, devices and search intent instead of only sitewide traffic.
  3. Separate indexing from ranking. Confirm whether pages remain indexed before calling it an algorithm hit.
  4. Review spam-policy compliance. Check scaled content abuse, doorway abuse, expired domain abuse, cloaking, site reputation abuse, hidden text, misleading functionality and manipulative links.
  5. Audit low-information pages. Generic summaries and near-duplicate templates are increasingly easy to reproduce.
  6. Review AI/template publishing patterns. Publishing scale and repetition can matter more than whether copy “sounds AI.”
  7. Check technical basics. Canonicals, robots directives, internal links, rendering, sitemaps, speed and crawlability can cause losses that resemble algorithm changes.
  8. Compare competitors that gained. Look for stronger intent satisfaction, depth, evidence, freshness, usability, authority or format.

Core Update Recovery vs Spam Update Recovery

After a core update drop

Review usefulness, intent, originality, page experience, topical authority and whether competitors now satisfy the query better.

After a spam update drop

Start with Google's spam policies. Cosmetic rewriting may not be enough if the publishing, linking or site strategy itself is non-compliant.

Google notes that automated spam systems may take time to recognize long-term compliance after fixes, so recovery should not be judged after only a few days.

What Google's 2026 Direction Means for SEO

  • Original information matters more. Google is explicitly encouraging unique, non-commodity content for generative AI Search.
  • AI assistance is not automatically a problem. Low-value mass production and manipulation are.
  • Traditional SEO and AI Search are connected. Crawlability, indexing, relevance and quality remain foundational.
  • Topical expertise matters. Discover guidance reinforces topic-level expertise and depth.
  • Brand preference is becoming more visible. Preferred Sources now extends into generative Search experiences.
  • Some old SERP tactics are disappearing. FAQ rich results are a clear example.
  • Spam policies apply across the wider Search experience. That includes generative AI responses.

A Safer AI-Assisted Content Workflow for 2026

  1. Research the query, SERP and real user problem.
  2. Collect first-hand input from specialists, customers, sales calls or internal teams.
  3. Add original examples, screenshots, tests, case studies or data.
  4. Use AI for outlining, drafting, comparison and editing where it saves time.
  5. Fact-check claims that can change or influence a decision.
  6. Remove generic filler and duplicated explanations.
  7. Add relevant images and videos where they improve understanding.
  8. Publish only when the page has a reason to exist beyond targeting a keyword.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the latest Google update in 2026?

The latest confirmed rollout is the August 2026 spam update, which began August 18 and applies globally to all languages. As of August 21, it is still being reported as rolling out.

How many Google spam updates have there been in 2026?

Three have been publicly announced so far: March, June and August 2026.

How many broad core updates has Google released in 2026?

Two broad Search core updates have been announced so far: March 2026 and May 2026. Google also released a separate Discover core update in February.

Does Google penalize AI-generated content?

Google does not define content as spam simply because AI helped create it. Scaled, unoriginal or manipulative content created primarily to influence Search can violate Google's spam policies regardless of how it was produced.

Does llms.txt improve Google rankings or AI Overview visibility?

Google says llms.txt is not required for Google Search and does not positively or negatively affect Search visibility or rankings.

Is FAQ schema still useful?

FAQ content can still be useful, but Google stopped showing the FAQ rich-result feature in 2026. Do not implement FAQ markup solely in expectation of the old expandable SERP result.
